# Ops 07 — Banking & Cash-Flow Readiness

Companion to ops/05-booking-flow.md. Why the business HAS a purpose-built account and how to
open it on business credit alone. Criteria-only — no personal underwriting facts belong here.

## Account criteria (hard requirements)

- **No ChexSystems screening.** The account provider must not open a ChexSystems report for
  this business. Providers verified 2026 to skip ChexSystems: **Mercury** (free checking/savings,
  free ACH + wires, no minimums; fintech, partner banks Choice Financial / Column N.A., FDIC),
  **Bluevine** (highest-interest business checking), **Novo** (digital-first, fast), **Lili**
  (freelancer/sole-prop style). Confirm each provider's current terms at application — they may use
  **Early Warning Services / TeleCheck** instead, which is an identity/compliance check, not a
  personal-history veto.
- **EIN-first underwriting.** Open the account in the LLC's name with its **EIN** (IRS, free).
  No hard pull of personal credit for the checking account itself.
- **Defer personal-pull products** (most business credit CARDS, business lines of credit) until
  any open personal credit matters resolve — those pull personal FICO and would also drag
  business financing through the same door.

## Order of operations

1. **EIN** — IRS EIN for Mark J Russell LLC (free, immediate). Required by every step below;
   Nav also keys business credit tracking to it.
2. **Open the business checking account** — Mercury first (middleman money movement: free ACH
   in from renters, out to owners; wires free; statements clean). Bluevine/Novo as alternates.
3. **Institutionalize Nav** — the business's credit observatory. Track D&B PAYDEX, Experian
   Intelliscore, Equifax Business monthly; verify every tradeline actually reports to the business
   file.
4. **Build the first EIN-only tradelines** — 2–3 Net-30 vendor accounts then **Nav Prime**
   (underwritten on business cash flow, not personal FICO). On-time history is the whole game;
   early-pay vendors that report to all three bureaus.
5. **Keep business and personal finances fully separate** — dedicated business account for all
   business flows; personal accounts never receive or disburse booking money. Protects liability
   AND keeps the business file clean.

Expected ramp: ~a few months of tradeline history before financing leans on the business instead
of a personal guarantee. Until then a personal guarantee on any credit product is normal — not a
reason to skip the build.

## Money-handling duty of care

This business holds full stay payments then disburses owner payouts — deposit-adjacent trust
work. Non-negotiable:

- **One pot:** renters pay the business account; owners are paid FROM it (ops/05 "Money rules").
  Never move booking funds through a personal account — that is co-mingling and breaks the
  middleman structure and its records.
- **Cash-flow state machine per booking:** receivable → held → settlement → closed. The ledger
  (ops/06) row and the bank statement must reconcile at each state.
- **Reserve buffer:** hold a minimum reserve in the pot (suggest: one typical booking's hold)
  for damage claims and cancellation refunds so settlements never dip negative.
- **Verify payout routing** (owner account details confirmed at onboarding, Form 03 §4) before
  the FIRST disbursement to any owner, ever.
- **Reconcile monthly:** ledger total vs bank balance vs Nav-reported cash flow. Any gap is a
  stop-bookings flag.

## Watch-outs

- Provider terms change; at application ask explicitly: "Do you screen ChexSystems (or EWS/
  TeleCheck), and do you hard-pull personal credit for a business checking account?" — get the
  yes/no in writing before submitting.
- Identity verification is normal at these providers and does not consult personal credit.
- Never pay for an EIN or a DUNS number — both have free paths; anyone charging is a red flag.
- No personal credit history, ChexSystems record, or fraud-case status needs to be disclosed to a
  provider screening EIN + identity + business activity. The business stands on its own file.
